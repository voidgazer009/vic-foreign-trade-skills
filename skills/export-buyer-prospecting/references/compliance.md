# Cold email compliance quick guide (not legal advice)

| Market | Rule | What the email must do |
|---|---|---|
| US | CAN-SPAM Act | Honest subject and sender; valid postal address; clear opt-out; honour opt-outs within 10 business days |
| EU (general) | GDPR + national ePrivacy laws | B2B email to a role-relevant work address can rely on legitimate interest in many member states; say who you are and where you got the address on request; easy objection; delete on request |
| Germany, Austria | UWG §7 (DE), TKG (AT) | Prior consent needed for email advertising, **even B2B**. Safer first touch: LinkedIn, phone, or a strictly individual inquiry |
| UK | PECR + UK GDPR | Corporate subscribers (limited companies) can be emailed without consent; sole traders and partnerships need consent; always offer opt-out |
| Canada | CASL | Implied consent if the address is conspicuously published, not marked "no unsolicited email", and the message is relevant to the person's role; include sender ID, address, unsubscribe |
| Australia | Spam Act 2003 | Consent (inferred if published conspicuously and relevant), identify sender, functional unsubscribe |

## Always
- Real name, real company, real postal address in every email.
- One-step opt-out ("Reply 'no thanks'") and stop immediately when used.
- Keep a do-not-contact list and check it before every send.
- Never buy scraped lists of personal (non-work) emails.

## Sanctions and export control
Before quoting buyers in sanctions-sensitive markets (the keyword file marks them `region_risk`), screen the company and its owners against OFAC SDN (US), EU consolidated list, and UN lists, and check the product is not dual-use. If in doubt, tell the user to consult a trade-compliance adviser before proceeding.
