# Outreach playbook

## The one-sentence test
Before writing, finish this sentence: "I'm writing to {name} because {specific fact about them}, and we can help with {specific outcome}." If you can't, research more or downgrade to a tier-B template.

## Structure of email 1
1. **Subject** (≤ 60 chars): their world, not yours. "Insulated bottles for your autumn catalogue" beats "Quotation of stainless bottles".
2. **Opening line**: the fact you found. Never "I hope this finds you well".
3. **Who we are, in one line**: product + one concrete spec + one confirmed proof (certification, MOQ, lead time, market served).
4. **CTA**: one low-effort question: sample, price range, a 10-minute call. Never "please check our catalogue".
5. **Signature**: name, title, company, postal address, opt-out line.

## Sequence timing
Day 0 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 14, then stop. Send Tue–Thu, 08:30–10:30 buyer local time (time zones are in the keyword file under `countries.*.tz`). Reply within 4 working hours when they answer.

## Language
- English works for most markets. Write in the buyer's language when their website has no English version (common: FR, ES/LATAM, IT, JP, KR, BR). Keep the same structure.
- Avoid Chinglish markers buyers filter on: "Dear Sir/Madam", "We are a professional manufacturer", "high quality and competitive price", "Hope to cooperate with you".

## Personalisation hooks, best first
1. A product line they launched or a brand they added
2. A trade show they exhibited at or visited
3. A job opening for buyers/category managers
4. A market change affecting them (new EU regulation, tariff change, freight cost shift)
5. Their customer reviews complaining about a product issue you solve

## After a reply
- "Send catalogue": send 1 page with 5–8 items most relevant to their range, not the full PDF.
- "Price?": give a range with Incoterm (FOB/CIF/DDP), MOQ, and lead time; ask target price and annual volume.
- "Not now": ask when their next sourcing cycle is; set a reminder.
- "We have a supplier": ask what they would improve with their current supplier; offer a sample for comparison.

## Benchmarks to set expectations (2026 industry studies)
- Average B2B cold email reply rates sit around 1–3.5% depending on how they are counted (Instantly 2026 report 3.43%, Woodpecker median 1.5%); positive replies are a fraction of that.
- Signal-based emails (a real trigger: launch, expansion, trade show, hiring) report 5–18% replies versus 1–1.5% for template blasts (papaverAI, 2026).
- In 4-email sequences, emails 2–4 bring most of the replies (B2B Data Index, 2026: 62%), so a single email understates results.
- Morning sends (buyer time, 8 am–noon) replied best in Belkins' 2025 data.
Treat these as direction, not promises. Judge the user's own numbers with `scripts/pipeline.py stats` after 30+ contacts.
